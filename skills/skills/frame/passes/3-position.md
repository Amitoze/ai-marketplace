# Pass 3 — Position

**Goal:** place the problem in its real landscape — what people
currently hire for this job, where each falls short, and the gap stated
against a named rival rather than a vacuum.

**Sits on:** Jobs-to-be-Done (Christensen/Moesta — competitors are
whatever people currently hire, including non-consumption) · Dunford,
*Obviously Awesome* (positioning starts from real alternatives) ·
Ulwick's outcomes as the scoring rubric.

## Output (dossier §3)

- **The hired-alternatives list:** everything people currently reach
  for when the problem occurs — products, workarounds, other humans,
  and explicitly "nothing / endure it". Non-consumption is an
  alternative and often the market leader.
- **Where each falls short**, scored against the gap as currently
  understood (revisit after Pass 4 formalises the outcome statements if
  the scoring feels loose).
- **The gap in one sentence, relative to a named alternative** — "X,
  which [named rival] cannot do because [reason]" — never "no one is
  doing this".

## Process

1. **Anchor in incidents, not categories.** "The last time this problem
   occurred for someone, what did they reach for?" — pulls real
   alternatives; "who are the competitors?" pulls a category list.
2. **Force "nothing" onto the list** and take it seriously: what does
   enduring the problem cost, and why is that price being paid today?
   (This is the holding-forces answer from Pass 2, seen from the
   market side.)
3. **Score honestly.** Each alternative gets what it does WELL before
   what it lacks — a landscape of straw men is a reframed vacuum.
4. **State the gap vs a named rival.** If no rival can be named, the
   usual reason is that the problem isn't real (return to Pass 2), not
   that the idea is unprecedented.
5. **Solution-in-hand check applies double here:** if a pre-existing
   solution is on the alternatives list, score it as coldly as the
   rest.

## Question bank

- Last time this happened, what did they actually use or do?
- What are people paying today — in money, time, or endurance?
- What does the current best alternative do genuinely well?
- Who has tried to solve this before and stopped? Why?
- If this solution vanished tomorrow, what would its users go back to?

## Exit check

§3 lists ≥3 real alternatives including "nothing", each with a
strength and a shortfall, and closes with a one-sentence gap naming a
specific rival. Claims about alternatives carry evidence tags like any
other world-fact.
