# Pass 5 — De-risk

**Goal:** treat the gap between current and desired state as a causal
structure, not a distance — name every assumption the bet rests on,
rank them, price the cheapest tests, define the kill condition, and
render the verdict.

**Sits on:** Torres, assumption testing (riskiest first; a failed test
is a cheap save) · Michie et al. 2011, COM-B / Behaviour Change Wheel
(*Implementation Science*) · theory-of-change tradition (Weiss,
evaluation literature) · Moesta's four forces of progress · Newell &
Simon, problem space (operators and path constraints).

## Output (dossier §5)

- **The bridge:** the causal chain from intervention to desired state,
  with EVERY arrow numbered as an assumption (a1, a2, …). The chain is
  only as strong as its least-evidenced arrow.
- **COM-B diagnosis:** is the gap a Capability, Opportunity, or
  Motivation deficit? One classification, defended. It checks the
  intervention *category* — stronger validation than a solution
  fitting a problem written after it.
- **Moves and constraints:** the operators available (all the ways the
  gap could in principle be crossed — including do-nothing) and the
  path constraints (moves ruled out, and why).
- **Crossing forces:** push of the situation, pull of the new, anxiety
  of the new, habit of the present. Anxiety and habit are the ones
  first drafts omit; they are where adoption dies.
- **Ranked assumption register:** risk × load-bearing, riskiest first.
  Includes the auto-promotions (below).
- **Cheapest test for the top three** — each answerable in days, not
  phases; interview questions count as tests.
- **The kill condition:** the specific evidence that would make us drop
  this, written before any test runs.
- **Verdict:** OPEN → VALIDATED / KILLED / PARKED, dated, one line why.

## Process

1. **Build the bridge** from §2's current state to §4's desired state;
   number every arrow.
2. **Auto-promote:** every load-bearing claim still tagged
   `[my-synthesis]` in §2 becomes an assumption in the register. The
   evidence discipline and the risk register are the same machine seen
   twice — no exceptions.
3. **Classify with COM-B** and check the solution category matches
   (capability deficits → education/training-shaped interventions;
   opportunity → environmental restructuring; motivation → persuasion/
   incentive). A mismatch is a red flag on the whole bet.
4. **Name the four forces**, pressing hardest on anxiety ("what makes
   the person avoid the solution even if it works?") and habit.
5. **Rank, price, and set the kill condition** BEFORE testing — a kill
   condition written after results arrive is a rationalisation.
6. **Render the verdict** and route the hand-off: VALIDATED → /roadmap;
   KILLED/PARKED → DECISIONS.md with the concrete reopening condition.

## Question bank

- For each arrow: what evidence says this link holds? What breaks it?
- What must be true about the USER of the solution, not just the
  solution?
- Why would someone who has this problem still not adopt the fix?
- Which single assumption, if false, kills the whole bet?
- What result from the next interview would make you drop this?

## Exit check

Every arrow numbered and in the register; one COM-B classification
defended; top three assumptions each have a cheapest test; the kill
condition is concrete enough that a disinterested reader could apply
it; the verdict line is filled in and the hand-off routed.
